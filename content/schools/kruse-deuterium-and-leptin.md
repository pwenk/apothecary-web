In the glass of water next to you, roughly one hydrogen atom in every 6,400 is twice as heavy as the rest. You've been drinking them since birth. So did every animal that ever lived.

Jack Kruse thinks those heavy atoms are grit in the smallest motor in your body. By the end of this page you'll know what that motor is, the one FDA-approved drug that uses heavy hydrogen on purpose, and why the breakfast plan Kruse wrote in 2011 works for a reason he didn't name.

## Hydrogen's heavy twin

An ordinary hydrogen atom is one proton and one electron. **Deuterium** carries a neutron as well, which doubles its weight without changing its chemistry much. Harold Urey found it in 1931 and won a Nobel Prize for it three years later. Seawater holds about 150 parts per million.

Doubling the weight does change one thing: speed. A bond to deuterium breaks more slowly than a bond to ordinary hydrogen, and a deuterium nucleus hops between molecules more slowly. Chemists call this the kinetic isotope effect, and it is solid, textbook physics.

Drug makers use it. In 2017 the FDA approved deutetrabenazine, a Huntington's disease drug in which some hydrogens were swapped for deuterium. The liver breaks the heavier bonds more slowly, so the drug lasts longer and needs fewer doses. Heavy hydrogen is real enough to sell.

## Kruse's jammed motor

Inside each mitochondrion sits **ATP synthase**, a protein turbine. Protons flow through it, spin its rotor, and each turn presses out ATP, the cell's energy coin. Paul Boyer and John Walker shared a Nobel Prize in 1997 for working out how it turns. It is a genuine rotary engine, about ten millionths of a millimetre across.

Kruse's claim: deuterium rides along with the protons, and because it is heavier and slower, it jams the turbine and cracks it. Think of a fine watch with sand in the gears. A follower on X summed up the model as keeping the rotor spinning near 9,000 rpm "without kinetic jam."

From there Kruse builds outward. Burn fat instead of sugar, because water made from burning fat is lighter. Get sunlight and cold to keep the motor clean. Drink deuterium-depleted water, or DDW. Asked which supplements he recommends, he answered in three words: DDW is it. Asked whether some habit was linked to deuterium, he wrote, "Everything about humans is linked to deuteration or lack thereof."

Some of the pieces are true. Fats are lighter in deuterium than body water, a known quirk of isotope chemistry. The rest is untested: no one has shown that the deuterium in normal water damages ATP synthase in a living person, or that lowering it by a fraction improves health.

## The Hungarian evidence

Most of the human data trace back to Hungary. In 1993 Gábor Somlyai and colleagues reported in *FEBS Letters* that cells grew more slowly in deuterium-depleted water, and grew normally again when deuterium was added back. Their reading: cells use natural deuterium as a growth signal, and cancer cells might be slowed by removing it.

The clinical follow-up is small. A 2008 report described four patients with lung cancer that had spread to the brain who drank DDW alongside treatment. Retrospective case series and a small randomized trial in prostate cancer followed, mostly from the same circle of researchers. There is no large, independent, randomized trial showing DDW extends life in any cancer.

One more fact for scale. Animals get sick from heavy water only when it replaces roughly a fifth of their body water, more than a thousand times the natural level. Whether trimming the last few parts per million matters to a human is an open question. **DDW is a plausible lab idea with thin human evidence**, and its real risk is a person choosing it instead of treatment.

## Leptin, the fat that talks

Now the breakfast. In 1994 Jeffrey Friedman's lab at Rockefeller University found the gene behind a strain of hugely obese mice. It coded for a hormone made by fat cells. They named it **leptin**, from the Greek for thin.

Leptin is your fat reporting to your brain. More fat, more leptin, and the hypothalamus relaxes: appetite eases, the thyroid and fertility stay switched on. Diet hard and leptin falls within days, faster than the fat itself, and the brain answers with hunger and a lower thermostat.

When a girl born unable to make leptin received injections, reported in 1999, she ate less and lost large amounts of fat. Hopes ran high for ordinary obesity. Then the same year a trial of leptin injections in typical obese adults gave only modest weight loss at the highest doses. Most obese people already make plenty of leptin. Their brains have stopped listening, a state called **leptin resistance**. It's a smoke alarm ringing in a house where everyone has gone deaf.

## The reset

Kruse's 2011 "Leptin Prescription" aimed at that deafness. If you're overweight, he wrote, you're leptin resistant, full stop. The fix: eat a large breakfast of protein and fat soon after waking, with few carbs. Never snack, "initially and forever." Eat three meals, later two. Leave hours between dinner and sleep, and make the house dark after sunset. Within weeks, he promised, cravings vanish. He called it brain surgery without a blade.

What did trials find? In 2022 Alexandra Johnstone's group in Aberdeen gave 30 adults with overweight or obesity two four-week diets with identical calories: one loaded at breakfast, one at dinner. Weight loss was the same, and so was energy burned. **The big-breakfast weeks came with clearly less hunger.** An earlier Israeli trial had reported more weight loss with big breakfasts; the tighter Aberdeen study didn't reproduce it.

So the loop closes like this. Kruse's breakfast probably works, when it works, because a protein-heavy morning meal quiets appetite for the day, making fewer calories easy. Nobody has tested whether it "resets" leptin. His other diagnostic claims, such as a raised reverse T3 as proof of leptin resistance in fit people, have no trial behind them.

> Kruse's breakfast works the way grandmothers said it would: a full morning makes a quiet afternoon.

## What holds up, what doesn't

- **Holds:** deuterium is real, heavier and slower; the kinetic isotope effect is textbook, and drug makers use it.
- **Thin:** DDW slowing cancer, based on cell studies and small human series from one research circle.
- **Untested:** natural deuterium jamming ATP synthase as a cause of disease.
- **Holds:** leptin reports fat stores to the brain; leptin resistance is real in common obesity.
- **Partly holds:** a big protein breakfast cuts hunger; equal-calorie trials show no extra weight loss.
- **Untested:** that the leptin reset "rewires" the hypothalamus.

## Before you try anything

Deuterium-depleted water is expensive water. It is no substitute for cancer treatment, and an oncologist should hear about it before you start. Big shifts in carbs and meal timing change blood sugar fast: if you take insulin or other diabetes drugs, or have kidney disease, get a doctor involved first.
