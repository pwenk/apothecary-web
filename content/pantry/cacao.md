In 1981, two New York psychiatrists, Donald Klein and Michael Liebowitz, suggested that the rush of falling in love comes from a brain chemical called phenylethylamine, PEA. They also floated why heartbroken people reach for chocolate: it contains PEA. The press turned this into "the chocolate theory of love", and it has never really left.

There is PEA in chocolate. You just never get to feel it. By the end you'll know where it goes, and which of chocolate's molecules does reach your brain.

## Macro: a dense, fatty treat

An ounce of 70 to 85% dark chocolate, about two or three squares, carries 170 kcal. Most of that is fat, 12 g, from cocoa butter. You also get 13 g of carbohydrate, about 7 g of it sugar, plus 3 g of fiber and 2 g of protein.

The darker the bar, the less sugar and the more cocoa solids, which is where nearly everything interesting lives. Milk chocolate trades much of that for sugar and milk. So "chocolate" covers anything from a near-medicinal bitter brick to candy.

## Micro: a surprising mineral bar

Dark chocolate's micro view embarrasses many vegetables. One ounce brings **56% of your copper**, 24% of manganese, 19% of iron and 15% of magnesium. Copper helps you make red blood cells and the pigment melanin; magnesium is involved in hundreds of enzymes, including the ones that relax muscle.

All of it comes from the bean itself. Cacao is a seed, and seeds pack minerals to feed the plant that will sprout from them. More cocoa, more minerals.

> Chocolate's love chemical dies at the door, and the caffeine cousin walks right in.

## The rest: theobromine, the "love molecule", and the bliss chemical

**Theobromine** is the bar's real drug: about 227 mg per ounce, with just 23 mg of caffeine alongside. The name comes from the plant, *Theobroma cacao*, which Linnaeus named "food of the gods". Theobromine is caffeine's cousin, a milder, slower stimulant that also works by blocking adenosine, the brain's tiredness signal, and it relaxes blood vessels and airways a little.

Dogs break theobromine down very slowly, so it builds up. The Merck Veterinary Manual lists mild signs from about 20 mg per kilo of body weight and seizures from about 60. For a small dog, a bar of dark chocolate is a medical emergency.

**PEA**, the love molecule, closes our loop. Chocolate contains small amounts, but an enzyme called MAO-B, found in your gut and liver, destroys PEA almost as fast as it arrives. Very little reaches your brain. Later studies failed to confirm the Klein–Liebowitz idea, and whatever chocolate does for heartbreak, it isn't PEA doing it.

**Anandamide** is the next contender. Its name comes from Sanskrit *ananda*, bliss, and it binds the same receptors as cannabis. In 1996 Daniele Piomelli's lab reported in *Nature* that chocolate contains anandamide and related molecules. The amounts are tiny, and nobody has shown they produce any effect from a normal portion.

**Flavanols** such as epicatechin are the health story: about 24 mg per ounce in USDA flavonoid data for dark chocolate. They help blood vessels widen and have been studied in heart and brain trials. Heavy processing, especially "Dutching" with alkali, strips much of them out.

And then there's the most famous chocolate graph of all. In 2012 the cardiologist Franz Messerli showed in the *New England Journal of Medicine* that countries eating more chocolate win more Nobel prizes per person. It was a tongue-in-cheek lesson about correlation, with Switzerland near the top of both lists.

## In the Apothecary's opinion

A small square of dark chocolate is a fine pleasure with real minerals and a mild stimulant in it. The love molecule is a myth; the theobromine buzz is real. Treat it as a treat with benefits, at 170 kcal an ounce.

Keep every bar away from dogs. If caffeine keeps you up, remember dark chocolate counts. Some dark chocolates have tested high for lead and cadmium, so brand variety helps.

Chocolate, coffee and the dopamine you met in the banana all circle one idea: the brain chemistry of attention, reward and drive. For some brains, that system runs differently from the start, and people quietly self-medicate with stimulants for years without knowing why. On to [ADHD](/signals/adhd).
