The most reliable way to drop a healthy man's sperm count to zero is a drug sold as making him more of a man. In 1990 the World Health Organization tested it as a contraceptive on 271 fertile men, and it worked.

By the end of this page you'll know why, and why that same hormone has to become estrogen inside a man's body before he can fully want sex.

Start where every steroid starts: a molecule with a bad reputation.

## Built from cholesterol

Every testosterone molecule begins as cholesterol. Inside the Leydig cells of the testes, a carrier protein called StAR ferries cholesterol into the mitochondria, the cell's power plants. There an enzyme snips off its tail and leaves **pregnenolone**, the mother of all steroid hormones.

From pregnenolone the factory has two assembly lines. One runs through DHEA and androstenedione. The other runs through progesterone, then androstenedione. Both end at testosterone. A few enzymes over, the same raw material becomes cortisol, aldosterone and the estrogens. One kitchen, many dishes.

Jamal Dinkoui, the Berbarian Wizard, makes a point in his *Testosterone Kabbalah* that surprises the steak-and-butter crowd: the cell can build its own cholesterol from carbohydrate, fat or protein, so making testosterone doesn't require a high-fat diet. Transport into the mitochondria and the signal to start are the usual bottlenecks.

That signal comes from above.

## The order from the brain

The hypothalamus fires a hormone called GnRH in pulses. Each pulse tells the pituitary to release two messengers. LH, luteinising hormone, tells the Leydig cells to make testosterone. FSH tells the Sertoli cells, the testes' nursery staff, to raise sperm.

Testosterone and estradiol then report back up and slow the pulses, like a thermostat that turns the boiler down once the room is warm. Remember that thermostat. It explains the whole TRT story later.

Inside the testes the concentration is enormous. When Andrea Coviello's team at the University of Washington sampled testicular fluid directly in 2005, blood testosterone was just 1.2 percent of the level inside the testis. Sperm production depends on that local flood.

Once testosterone leaves the testes, it has three possible futures.

## Two daughters and a chaperone

**DHT.** The enzyme 5-alpha-reductase converts some testosterone into dihydrotestosterone. DHT grips the androgen receptor harder than testosterone and can never become estrogen. Before birth it builds the penis and prostate. In adults it drives beard growth, prostate growth and, on a genetically sensitive scalp, hair loss. Finasteride blocks the type-2 form of the enzyme. Dutasteride blocks types 1 and 2.

**Estradiol.** The enzyme aromatase, plentiful in fat tissue, converts testosterone into estradiol, the main estrogen. Men need it. In 2013 Joel Finkelstein's group at Massachusetts General Hospital used a drug to switch off the natural hormone production of about 400 healthy men, then added back testosterone at different levels for 16 weeks. Half also got an aromatase blocker. Muscle and strength followed testosterone. Fat gain followed missing estrogen. Desire and erections needed both. Men whose bodies can't make estrogen at all grow tall with brittle bones.

Jamal draws the practical line in his estrogen essay: very high estradiol can matter in context, but driving it as low as possible harms bone, blood fats and sexual function. In his words elsewhere, indiscriminate aromatase suppression is a biological mistake.

> A man's desire runs partly on estrogen, built from his own testosterone.

**SHBG.** Most testosterone in blood rides bound to proteins. Sex hormone-binding globulin, made by the liver, holds it tight. Albumin holds it loosely. Only a small fraction swims free at any moment. Obesity, insulin resistance and an underactive thyroid tend to lower SHBG. Too much thyroid hormone, liver disease and estrogen tend to raise it.

Jamal's SHBG guide makes the arithmetic point the supplement ads skip. A man with total testosterone of 20 and one percent free has the same free amount as a man at 10 with two percent free. **A higher free percentage doesn't prove more hormone.** Direct free-testosterone tests are also unreliable. Reference labs use equilibrium dialysis or a validated calculation from total testosterone, SHBG and albumin.

All three futures depend on how much was made, and that depends on the clock.

## The daily tide

Testosterone rises after you fall asleep, reaches a high around the first dream (REM) phase and stays up until waking. It's highest in the morning and drifts down through the day. That's why the Endocrine Society asks for two separate morning fasting blood tests before anyone diagnoses low testosterone.

Sleep is the lever most men pull without noticing. In 2011 Rachel Leproult and Eve Van Cauter at the University of Chicago restricted ten healthy young men to five hours a night for a week. Their daytime testosterone fell by 10 to 15 percent. Jamal puts sleep in the second gate of his restoration plan, right after confirming the number is real.

The other common suppressors:

- **Obesity.** Fat tissue turns more testosterone into estrogen, insulin resistance lowers SHBG, and the brain's signal weakens. Weight loss tends to reverse it.
- **Opioids.** They suppress GnRH. Low testosterone is common in long-term users.
- **High prolactin, illness, steroid medicines.** Each turns the brain's signal down. (Prolactin has [its own essay](/apothecary/prolactin).)

And then there's the suppressor men pay for.

## TRT and the empty factory

Inject testosterone and the thermostat reads a warm room. GnRH slows, LH and FSH collapse, and the testes go quiet. In Coviello's 2005 study, three weeks of weekly injections pushed LH and FSH down to a few percent of normal and cut testosterone inside the testes by 94 percent, while blood levels rose. More in the blood, almost none where sperm is made.

The 1990 WHO trial measured what follows. Of 271 fertile men on weekly testosterone injections across ten centres, 65 percent had no sperm at all within six months. In the following year of using that as their only contraception, there was one pregnancy. After stopping, sperm counts took a median of about four months to recover to a normal level.

In other words, TRT works on the testes like a contraceptive. The Endocrine Society tells doctors not to prescribe it to men planning children soon. Jamal writes the same: exogenous testosterone is not a fertility-preserving way to "restore" natural production, and a man who may want children should say so before he starts. Recovery after stopping usually happens, though after years of use it can be slow.

The *Testosterone Kabbalah* (August 2026) is a follow-up to a first version he says reached almost ten million views. Its core claim: a blood number is the visible output of a timed system, so find the limiting layer before pushing the number. His order runs from confirming the result, to sleep and light, to food and body fat, to removing suppressors (drugs, porn, overtraining), to retesting, and only then to the medical branch. He also reproduces Ray Peat's lists of things that help or block steroid production, and labels them as Peat's model with very uneven evidence.

- **Holds up (strong):** the cholesterol-to-testosterone chain, LH control, DHT and estradiol as active daughters, men's need for estrogen, the morning peak, sleep loss, obesity and opioids lowering testosterone, TRT shutting down sperm production.
- **Holds up (reasonable):** Jamal's order of operations, which matches the guideline logic of confirming and finding causes first.
- **Mixed to fringe:** Peat's longer lists of promoters and blockers, and most "booster" supplements in men with normal levels.

## Before you try anything

Get two morning fasting tests with LH, FSH, SHBG, estradiol and prolactin, so a doctor can tell whether the brain or the testes are the weak link. If children are anywhere in your future, say it before any testosterone, SERM or hCG decision. Aromatase blockers without a clear reason can cost you bone and desire.

Remember the shared kitchen: the same pregnenolone that becomes testosterone, a few enzymes over, becomes a hormone that peaks every morning before your eyes open. It's blamed for belly fat and burnout, and one of its popular diagnoses doesn't exist. Next: cortisol.
