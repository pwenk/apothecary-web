For a few months now, a strange genre has been washing across X. The posts are translated from Chinese, written with the calm of a grandmother passing down a recipe, and nearly all begin with the same two words: "Sex Tips:".

Warm her feet first. Slow down before you speed up. Squeeze at the right moment and the climax happens with nothing lost.

Some of these are two thousand years old. One of them, the squeeze, has a side effect the posts never mention. By the end of this page you'll know which tips the science backs, which belong in a museum, and what that squeeze actually does.

## A very old manual

In 168 BCE a noble family in Mawangdui, in what's now Hunan, sealed a tomb that held silk manuscripts on breathing, diet and sex. Later texts filled out the tradition, above all the **Sunü jing**, the *Classic of the Pure Woman*, probably written between the late Han dynasty and the Three Kingdoms period.

The book is a dialogue. The Yellow Emperor asks questions; Sunü, a goddess and teacher, answers. Its original Chinese text was lost, and it survives mainly because a Japanese physician, Tamba Yasuyori, copied large parts of it into his medical collection, the *Ishinpō*, in 984.

Its worldview runs on **jing**, the body's essence, which the Taoists believed was carried partly in semen. Spend it carelessly and you age; conserve it and you nourish your health and spirit. From that one idea flow most of the tips now trending in translation.

## Nine shallow, one deep

The most quoted rhythm from the old texts is "nine shallow, one deep." Many shallow movements, then one deep one, repeated in cycles.

Read clinically, it's a pacing tool. Shallow movement is less stimulating for the man and sits where much of a woman's nerve supply lives, near the entrance. **The rhythm slows his rise while building her anticipation.** One of the modern "Sex Tips" accounts says almost the same thing in plain language: lasting longer is mostly about keeping stimulation from running too hot from the start.

Western sexology arrived at the same idea in 1956, when the American urologist James Semans described the stop-start method: stimulation up to near the point of no return, then a pause until the urge fades, then again. Today we'd call it edging. Masters and Johnson later added the squeeze technique, a firm press just below the head of the penis during the pause, and both remain standard behavioural approaches for premature ejaculation.

So far, the grandmother and the urologist agree. They part ways at the squeeze.

## The famous squeeze

The tip doing the rounds says roughly this: just before he climaxes, press firmly at the base, and he'll have the orgasm without releasing anything. The Taoist version is older. Classical teachers, and in the 1980s and 1990s Mantak Chia in books like *The Multi-Orgasmic Man*, describe pressing on the perineum, the patch of skin between the scrotum and the anus, to keep the semen in.

Here's what actually happens. Pressing there closes off the urethra below the prostate. The contractions still fire, and the semen, with nowhere to go forward, goes backward into the bladder. **That's retrograde ejaculation**, and it leaves the body later with urine.

The Taoists believed the jing climbed up the spine to nourish the brain. It goes to the bladder. Doing this now and then is generally considered harmless, though pressing hard and often on that area isn't a great idea for the delicate tissue and nerves there. The essence-saving claim simply doesn't hold up. If you notice cloudy urine or dry orgasms without trying for them, that deserves a doctor's look, because retrograde ejaculation can also be a sign of nerve damage, prostate surgery or certain medicines.

> Nearly every good tip in this genre is the same tip: slow down and let the wanting grow.

## Orgasm without ejaculation

Chia's bigger claim is more interesting: that a man can learn to separate orgasm from ejaculation, using breath and control of the pelvic floor muscles to ride several orgasmic peaks without spilling. Evidence here is mostly personal testimony; nobody has tested it in a proper trial.

What does have evidence is the muscle work. In a 2014 Italian study, 40 men with lifelong premature ejaculation did 12 weeks of pelvic floor training, and 33 of them gained control, with average time to climax rising from about 40 seconds to well over two minutes. It was small and had no control group, so read it as a promising sign. The pelvic floor clearly takes part in the ejaculatory reflex, and training it changes the reflex.

## Why it switches off: prolactin and the refractory period

After a man climaxes, he enters the refractory period, a stretch when arousal is hard or impossible to restart. One of the "Sex Tips" posts notes how sensitive men are right after, and anyone who's tried to carry on knows the feeling.

For years the suspect was **prolactin, the hormone that says "enough"**, which surges after orgasm in both sexes. Stuart Brody and Tillmann Krüger pooled lab data in 2006 and found the prolactin rise after intercourse was about 400% larger than after masturbation, which they read as a sign of greater satisfaction.

Whether prolactin causes the refractory period is another matter. A 2021 study in mice raised and blocked prolactin around mating and found no change in the refractory period. The hormone clearly rises; its role in the off-switch is now in doubt. Our page on [prolactin](/apothecary/prolactin) goes deeper.

Women often have a shorter refractory period, or none, which is why another "Sex Tips" post coaches patience through the oversensitive seconds after a woman's orgasm toward a second wave. Masters and Johnson documented female multiple orgasms in the 1960s; the old texts knew it long before.

## The socks, and the other gentle tips

Warm her feet, says one popular post, because a Dutch brain-scanning lab found women in socks reached orgasm far more often. The story traces back to press interviews with Gert Holstege's team in Groningen around 2005. It's a lab anecdote that never became a trial, and the point behind it survives anyway: **a body that feels cold or unsafe holds back.**

Make her come first. Slow down. Pay full attention. Say something warm at the peak. None of these need a study. They're what any sexologist would say, dressed in a grandmother's voice.

## What holds up

The pacing ideas hold up: nine shallow and one deep, stop-start, edging and the classic squeeze below the head all slow a man's rise and build anticipation for both. Pelvic floor training holds up on small but real evidence. The semen-as-life-essence idea and the perineum press do not; the press redirects semen into the bladder and saves nothing.

## Before you try anything

If you have pain, blood in semen, or regular dry orgasms you didn't aim for, see a urologist. Persistent premature ejaculation is common and very treatable, so it's worth a proper conversation rather than a trick.
