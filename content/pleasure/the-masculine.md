It usually happens on a tired evening. She says one sentence, quietly or with a laugh, and it lands right on the bruise you thought nobody could see. Your money. Your father. The thing you said you'd finish last spring.

What you do in the next four seconds tells her more about you than every birthday present you've ever wrapped. Most men do one of two things, and both lose.

There's a third move. It involves a joke, and by the end of this page you'll know why the joke works when nothing else does.

## A vector with a body

Somebody on X wrote that having a soul means being a vector: an orientation you don't negotiate, values that point one way. Yohami quoted it and added one line: **a masculine soul is a vector.**

A vector has two parts, size and direction. Plenty of men have size, the energy and the appetite, and no direction, so the energy sprays. Direction is the purpose you'd keep pursuing if nobody clapped. David Deida's oldest instruction to men is that this purpose comes first, before the relationship, and that a woman can feel within minutes whether a man has one or is looking for her to become it.

This sounds cold until you watch it from her side. A man whose whole life orbits her puts the weight of his meaning on her shoulders. A man pointed at something larger lets her rest beside him without carrying anything.

Yohami's version is the cleanest: you commit to you. You're a force with direction and gravity. She's a wave, free to orbit you or not. Which raises the question of what keeps the orbit steady when the wave gets rough.

## Presence, the cheapest luxury

Presence is attention with nothing leaking out of it. No phone half-face-down on the table. No rehearsing your next line while she's still talking. Your eyes, your breath and your body all pointing at the same place.

It costs nothing and almost nobody has it, which is why it reads as status. A man who can sit fully in a room without needing the room to entertain or approve of him is rarer than a Rolex, and women clock it faster.

One of the Chinese "Sex Tips" accounts made the same point about bed: what makes a woman remember a night is rarely technique and nearly always whether his attention was wholly on her. Attention is the first thing she feels and the last thing she forgets.

Hold that, because presence gets tested hardest when it would be easiest to drop.

## Above your own weather

Yohami describes himself, in one breathless post, as happy and sad and full of life and shrinking, all for no reason, and calls that whole. Another time he wrote, for himself, that all feelings are wrong, then laughed that the thought was just another feeling.

That's the posture. **Being above your moods means feeling all of them without letting any of them drive.** Anger comes through like a truck on the motorway; you hear it, you feel the road shake, you don't climb in.

Yohami adds a twist worth keeping: your relationship with your own mind is your relationship with women, because the mind behaves like the feminine. It nags, doubts, projects, runs simulations. If you argue with every thought, you'll argue with every mood she brings home. If you can let a thought finish talking and stay where you are, you can do the same for her.

Which brings us back to that tired evening.

## When she tests

The jab is a test. Tests sound like attacks and work like questions. Underneath the words her body is asking something simple: *are you still there if I push here?*

Man number one collapses. He explains, defends, apologises, sulks. The answer he gives is: no, push there and I fold. Man number two hits back, colder and louder. His answer is: push there and I leave. Both teach her the floor is soft.

Joe Hudson, the coach, posted a Taoist saying that fits: when a sword comes at you, be the ocean. **The sword goes in and the ocean is still the ocean.** You let the line land, you feel it sting, you stay warm, and you don't move your frame an inch.

One account on X listed what bratty, testing women train in a man: banter, frame, non-reactivity, restraint, pacing. The phrasing is cheeky and the list is accurate. A test that meets an ocean stops being a test and turns into play.

> She pushes to find the wall, and when she finds it warm, she leans.

## Leading without a leash

Leadership in this sense looks like dancing. The lead offers the move with his hand on her back. He feels whether she comes. If she doesn't, he adjusts and offers the next one. Control is shoving her through the step whether she wants it or not, and every woman knows the difference through her skin.

Yohami says it in archer's terms: will is the throw, outcome is the response. You put full intent behind the dart, then let it land where it lands. **You lead the movement and release the result.** A man who needs the outcome to go his way is still asking permission, only louder.

So he proposes. He decides the restaurant, the route home, the moment to kiss. If she's on board, he rewards her with warmth and pulls her closer. If she's not, he shrugs, smiles, and keeps walking his way. Begging, Yohami notes, only makes the gap wider.

Consent sits under all of this the way a floor sits under a dance. You check it once, calmly, and you keep feeling for it the whole time through her body, which tells you more than any form would.

## The joke at the right second

Now the loop from the top. She lands the line on the bruise, and the third man laughs.

He laughs the way a man laughs when his ground didn't move: "Wow, straight for the jugular tonight," with a grin, a hand on her waist, and then he carries on making dinner. He heard her. He might even agree there's something to it, tomorrow, in daylight. Tonight he shows her the thing she was really asking about: that he's still standing, still warm, still pointed somewhere.

Yohami's first-date advice runs the same way. She should feel certain the evening will be fun, certain he won't take her too seriously, certain he might mispronounce her name till the end. Humour is the proof that he's above the moment. A man who can play while being pushed is a man she can stop guarding against.

That's the masculine at its smallest and strongest: presence, direction, a steady frame, and a laugh at the right second.

But a frame only matters if something opens inside it. What happens on her side when she finally stops checking, and why does letting go feel, to so many women, like coming home? That's the next page.
